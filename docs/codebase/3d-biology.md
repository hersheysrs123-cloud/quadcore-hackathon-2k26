# 3D studio — Biology scenes

> Part of the [SocraticOS codebase reference](../../CODEBASE_SUMMARY.md). Cell, enzyme, DNA, protein, eye, respiratory, reflex arc, muscles, transpiration, peristalsis, carbon cycle, food chain, pollination, pathogens, mitosis/meiosis, cardiac cycle.

3. **Biology Engine** (`BiologyCanvas.jsx`, `EyeCanvas.jsx`, `RespiratoryCanvas.jsx`, `ReflexArcCanvas.jsx`, `AntagonisticMusclesCanvas.jsx`, `TranspirationCanvas.jsx`, `PeristalsisCanvas.jsx`, `CarbonCycleCanvas.jsx`, `FoodChainPyramidCanvas.jsx`, `FlowerPollinationCanvas.jsx`, `BacteriaVsVirusCanvas.jsx`, `MitosisMeiosisCanvas.jsx`, `CardiacCycleCanvas.jsx`, `cell-organelles.jsx`, `arm-rig.jsx`, `tube-transit.jsx`, `ecosystem-diorama.jsx`, `flux-chart.jsx`, `timeline-kit.jsx` & `stage-stepper.jsx`):
   - **Plant & Animal Cell Explorer** (`cell`, `lib/cellBiology.js`): Nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts, rough and smooth ER, Golgi, ribosomes, lysosomes, centrioles, permanent vacuole, cell membrane and cellulose cell wall; the organelle key follows the cell type. Tonicity drives three plant states (turgid, flaccid, plasmolysed) and an animal cell that crenates when shrunk or swells and bursts when the membrane ruptures. Labels are callouts in two columns beside the cell (`PLANT_CALLOUTS` / `ANIMAL_CALLOUTS`, anchors in the scaled cell frame), not labels on each organelle, which piled up in one corner. A flaccid plant cell's membrane stays against the wall; it only pulls away past 0.85 × `PLASMOLYSIS_AT` (it used to shrink at any positive tonicity while the panel said "Flaccid"). `FitCamera` frames each cell type. The **Cutaway view** (`CutawayProvider` in `cell-organelles.jsx`) is one local clipping plane with its normal at −z: three keeps the side a clipping plane's normal points to, and the camera looks from +z, so this removes the front half and shows the sectioned organelles. It used to point at +z and slice off the hidden back half. Every callout anchor is on something drawn both with and without the cutaway: the outline of whatever crosses the cut (wall, membrane, nucleus, rough ER, vacuole) at z = 0, or an organelle behind it, clear of anything in front when the cell is whole. The Golgi (both cells), the centrioles and one lysosome were moved back onto the cut so the cutaway keeps them.
   - **Enzyme Kinetics & Denaturation** (`enzyme`, `lib/enzymes.js`): A globular enzyme whose active site is carved to the exact shape of its substrate. The body is a signed-distance function (a noisy sphere with a fine bumpy skin, minus the substrate's extruded outline plus 0.05 clearance), meshed once by `meshSdf` in `sdf-mesh.js`. The cleft has a floor behind the substrate and is open towards the camera: a closed slot faced sideways and the globule hid it. The substrate is an extruded key in two halves split along the bond, gold (`ENZYME_COLOURS.substrate`). It approaches, binds (the "enzyme–substrate complex" glows), splits, and the two amber products drift away while the enzyme is unchanged. The lower edge has a step, so it fits only one way up. Denaturation (`solveEnzyme().distortion`) writhes the surface as a smooth function of position, squeezes the pocket's lining shut, and tints the protein rose; a blocked substrate is turned away at the mouth. The old icosahedron wobbled each vertex by its index, so seam vertices separated and the surface cracked. The rate-against-temperature curve sits on a card below, with 0–80° ticks and staggered optimum and `DENATURE_TEMP` markers. `enzymeRate` is the Q10 ≈ 2 rise multiplied by the fraction of molecules still folded (a smoothstep from `UNFOLD_START` 31.78 °C over `DENATURE_WIDTH` 20 °C). `UNFOLD_START` is solved so the product peaks exactly at 37 °C, which gives the lopsided textbook curve: 9 % at 0 °C, 56 % at 45 °C, 7 % at 50 °C and 0 by 52 °C. pH is a Gaussian about 7. The "denatured enzyme" label and the key's colour follow `denatured || extremePh`, the same test as the active-site row, while `distortion` only drives the gradual squeeze. From 37 to 50 °C the chart shades a rose "denaturing" band, and the marker reads "fully denatured by 50 °C". `solveEnzyme().denaturing` (more than 5 % of the molecules unfolded but not yet `denatured`) turns the panel amber and makes `reversible` false: heat damage is permanent however little of it there is. A stage caption names what is happening. `FitCamera` frames the scene.
   - **DNA Double Helix Structure** (`dna`, `lib/dna.js`): A right-handed B-DNA helix (`helixPoint` uses z = −sin; the old z = +sin was left-handed) at 10.5 pairs a turn with a 135° minor groove. Each nucleotide is a sugar (pentagon plate in the strand colour), a phosphate (`BACKBONE_COLOURS.phosphate`) further out on the backbone rail, and a base on a short glycosidic link. Purines (A, G) are fused 6+5 ring plates and pyrimidines (C, T) single hexagons, each flat edge facing its partner, so every pair spans the same width. Two (A–T) or three (C–G) white hydrogen bonds (`HYDROGEN_BOND_COLOUR`) cross the gap, stretch as the fork opens, and fade out. Unzipping runs from the top and keeps each base on its own strand. Labels mark 5′/3′ on both strands (strand 1 reads 5′→3′ upwards), the backbone, and the pairing rule. `FitCamera` looks from a little above so the rings read as rings. The turn is paced by `speed` (Animation Speed) alone; the old `spin` slider duplicated it and is gone. The pairing caption sits 2.05 below the helix so it clears the strand-end labels.
   - **Protein Secondary Structure & Folding** (`protein`, `lib/proteinFolding.js`): The α-helix is right-handed (`helixPositions` uses z = −sin, as the DNA helix does; it used to be +sin, left-handed) at 100° and a rise of 0.62 per residue on a 0.95 radius, i.e. 3.6 residues a turn with the real rise-to-radius ratio. The backbone is a smooth `TubeGeometry` through the Cα atoms (`STRUCTURE_COLOURS.backbone`). Every residue has a side chain (R group) coloured by hydrophobicity, pointing outward from a helix axis, alternating above and below a β-sheet with the pleat, and in a random direction in the coil. Real proportions: α-helix rise 0.62 against a 0.95 radius (0.15 nm per residue, 0.23 nm radius, 0.38 nm Cα–Cα), and β-strands with 1.4 per residue and 2.0 between strands. The old rise of 0.42 squashed the helix flat. The coil is a persistent random walk of 1.57 steps, centred. The helix is laid on its side, N-terminus left and C-terminus right, and the scale is each conformation's own fit, blended by the fold fraction. The i→i+4 and cross-strand hydrogen bonds are dashed emerald.
   - **The Human Eye — Accommodation & Pupil Reflex** (`eye`): Anatomical and physiological cutaway eyeball simulation powered by `EyeCanvas.jsx` and `lib/eyeOptics.js`. Features dynamic ciliary accommodation, crystalline lens curvature morphing, pupil light reflex, Gullstrand equivalent power calculations, a spectacle-prescription control for short sight (negative dioptres) and long sight (positive) modelled as refractive rather than axial error — it moves the far or near point and grows the retinal blur circle until a correcting lens is worn — and a 10%–80% resizable sidebar HUD with edge drag bar and corner grip. The object-distance tag and the "focus behind/in front of retina" marker obey the labels toggle.
   - **Respiratory Mechanics & Thoracic Physics** (`respiratory`): Photorealistic 3D anatomical and physiological thoracic simulation powered by genuine clinical CT-derived thoracic skeleton (`skeleton_ct.glb`, 16.3 MB; isolated 24 ribs, T1–T12 thoracic vertebrae, L1–L3 lumbar crura anchors, sternum, xiphoid, and clavicles) with active bucket-handle lateral elevation and pump-handle AP sternal elevation, photorealistic medical lungs scan (`lung.glb`, 17.1 MB), external- vs internal-intercostal tension reported on live gauges from `lib/respiratory.js` (the intercostal muscle meshes themselves were removed to keep the scene light, and the three ribcage motion arrows were kept), muscular diaphragm dome morphing, dynamic airway particle stream vectors driven by Boyle's Law pressure gradients ($P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$), 3-state phase selector, live SVG physics gauges, and open-source model attribution modal. Anatomy labels are callouts in two columns (`ANATOMY_CALLOUTS`) with the lungs the right way round for the anterior view: the patient's right lung (3 lobes) is on the viewer's left, and the cardiac notch is on the viewer's right. The labels had them swapped. `FitCamera` frames `THORAX_VIEW`, shadows are off, the kinematic-arrow captions hide with the labels, and `showLabels` is read from params (it was local state).
   - **The Reflex Arc & Spinal Circuit** (`reflex_arc`): Forearm-in-a-flame withdrawal reflex on the shared arm rig's kinematics, drawn with our own anatomical arm: `ModelledArm` (`arm-model.jsx`, shared with the muscles scene) loads `public/models/arm.glb` (4.9 MB), built in Blender by `scripts/arm-model/` (no third-party mesh, so no credit). Bones: the clavicle and scapula (fixed), the humerus, and the ulna, radius, eight carpals, five metacarpals and fourteen phalanges. The finger segments are posed in the shader from `ARM_MODEL.hand`; this scene holds the `point` pose (index finger out, the others curled into the palm, the thumb opposed and folded over them). Muscles: the deltoid; supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, teres major and subscapularis, each domed over its fossa of the scapula and converging on its tendon; biceps (both heads and tendons); brachialis and the triceps' medial head, which wrap the front and back of the humerus (the bone is carved out of them), so no bone shows between biceps and triceps; coracobrachialis; the triceps' long and lateral heads and common tendon; brachioradialis and the forearm flexors and extensors, packed into the forearm's contour with fascial seams and their tendons kept as cords; the thumb's outcropping muscles; the thenar and hypothenar eminences, adductor pollicis and the interossei; the retinacula and the digital tendons. The bicipital aponeurosis and the triceps' tendon sheet are painted onto the muscles they lie on rather than built as plates. There is no skin. The soft tissue is stored at the rest pose with a `_bend` weight pair per vertex (humerus, forearm) and bent in the vertex shader: each vertex turns about the elbow by its share of the elbow angle, then about the shoulder by its share of the shoulder angle. Angles, not positions, are blended, so bends do not pinch. Every per-vertex value is blended across a muscle's heads (`soft.head_weights`), so where two heads merge the mesh cannot tear when it bends or morphs. Tendons that cross the elbow bend round it; the bellies stay put (the triceps belly is pinned to the humerus, and only its tendon follows the olecranon). The biceps and triceps have their own materials, which brighten with activation, and the biceps' `contract` morph shortens and swells its belly as the elbow flexes. The nerve routes were re-laid on the model's surface, measured in Blender: the sensory neuron runs up the palm side of the index finger, over the carpal tunnel, up the front of the forearm and the medial bicipital groove; the motor neuron runs down the medial face of the biceps to its end plates. The sensory route starts at the modelled index fingertip (`ARM_MODEL.indexTip`, generated). The arm sits in its own `<Suspense>`. Sensory (Aδ, 33 m/s), relay and motor (Aα, 90 m/s) neurons drawn as tubes hung on the bones and into `SpinalSegment`: a 3D length of the C6 cord (magnified; section-local `S(x, y, z)` with x dorsal→ventral, y lateral, z along the cord), cut square at the front, wider than it is deep as a real cervical cord is, notched by the anterior median fissure and posterior median sulcus, with the grey-matter butterfly (slender dorsal horns, broad ventral horns) standing just proud of the cut face, the central canal, the anterior and posterior spinal arteries and a vein along it, and the C6 vertebra (body, arch, spinous and transverse processes) behind the cut. Dorsal and ventral rootlets fan out of its side into the roots, the dorsal root swells into the ganglion, and the roots join into the spinal nerve (the vertebral body overlaps the arch and is as deep as it is tall; a thin body standing clear of the arch read as a loose plate); the sensory and motor fibres run together inside the nerve's translucent sheath (`NERVE_BUNDLE`) to the shoulder, as they do in a mixed nerve. Cell bodies (`Soma`): the sensory neuron's in the ganglion, the relay neuron's and the multipolar motor neuron's in the grey matter. The cord's parts are `Callout`s in two columns (`CORD_CALLOUTS`) inside a `LabelsOn` provider. The severed-root markers still sit at `DORSAL_ROOT_FRACTION` / `VENTRAL_ROOT_FRACTION` of each route, which now fall on the dorsal root just before the cord and the ventral root where it leaves it; the marker's background slab is only 0.32 deep so it cuts the root, not the cord's edge; a travelling action-potential bead with synapse flashes, driven by the physiological clock in `lib/reflexArc.js` (≈ 30 ms stimulus → effector). Controls: stimulus (sub-threshold 40 °C warmth on C fibres vs a flame that heats the skin to 80 °C), pathway (intact / severed dorsal root / severed ventral root), real-time vs 10× slow-motion. The scene reports its clock into params (`liveMs`, `liveStage`, `liveFired`) so the Details tab shows a live millisecond counter, per-stage milestone ticks and the biceps trigger. The candle stands on a light laminate table with legs. The dish sits 0.01 into the table top and the wax 0.01 into the dish; the dish's underside used to lie in the table top and z-fight. The candle has a melt pool and a wax run. Its label says the 80 °C is the skin's (`STIMULI.flame.label` is "Candle flame (skin heats to 80 °C)"), not the flame's. An outcome caption (`OUTCOME[solved.reason ?? "fires"]`) says what each run shows; mild warmth in particular (40 °C, below the ~43 °C pain threshold: slow C-fibre impulses, felt, no reflex) looked like nothing happening without it, and the candle's label follows the stimulus. The five stage milestones are one checklist on the right above the candle (clear of the cord callouts and the withdrawn hand), not labels that rode the arm onto the elbow. `FitCamera`, no shadows, and `showLabels` through a `Label` alias passed to `Cut`, `Candle` and `Milestone`, plus the `LabelsOn` provider for the callouts.
   - **Antagonistic Muscle Pairs & the Levered Elbow** (`antagonistic_muscles`): Vertical humerus, forearm swung by a 0–145° slider, dumbbell 0–25 kg, and a fatigue trigger. `lib/muscleMechanics.js` models the elbow as a third-class lever (load torque τ = W·L·sin θ, biceps moment arm 1.5–4.3 cm, length–tension curve, antagonist tone, tendon strain σ/E) and decides whether the biceps can hold the requested angle — under lactic-acid fatigue the arm gives way to the largest angle it can still hold and recovers as the acid clears. The arm is the reflex scene's modelled arm (`ModelledArm`, `arm-model.jsx`). With a load in it the hand eases from `relaxed` to `grip`, fingers round the handle and the thumb opposed over its top, and the dumbbell sits at the fitted grip (`ARM_MODEL.grip`, generated). The biceps' `contract` morph follows the elbow angle and its activation, the triceps' `stretch` morph draws it out over the flexed elbow, brachialis and brachioradialis bunch (`flex`), and the tendons walk from pearl to amber to rose as strain climbs. Live readouts: contracted/relaxed for both muscles, joint torque, biceps force and force ratio, capacity used, tendon strain, fatigue and max holdable load. Seen side-on (`MUSCLE_VIEW`, `FitCamera`), because the lever is a flat diagram in the arm's plane. The dark floor disc is gone. The dumbbell has rubber-coated plates with rims and hubs at moderate metalness (at 0.6–0.8 with no environment map they rendered black). Labels, force-vector symbols and the fatigue banner obey `showLabels`. `arm-rig.jsx` keeps only the shared kinematics (pose, frames, attachment points from `ARM_MODEL.landmarks`), the dumbbell and the joint markers.
   - **Plant Transpiration — Roots, Xylem & Stomata** (`transpiration`): A bean seedling (*Phaseolus vulgaris*) in a cut block of soil, and four numbered panels that follow the water through it, laid out like the insets of a textbook figure (numbered magnifier rings on the plant, arrows between the panels): **1 root hairs ×100** (a translucent root tip with its stele showing through, a root cap, a dense zone of hairs threading between sand, silt and clay–humus crumbs, each particle wrapped in a water film with menisci bridging the gaps; water `PathFlow`s run film → hair tip → hair → stele), **2 stem ×40** (a wedge cut from the stem: the ring of eight collateral bundles, xylem vessels split open along the cut faces as real grooves with annular and spiral thickenings, a split sieve tube with sieve plates, hooked hairs; `TubeFlow`s carry water up the vessels and sugar down the phloem, and an embolism bubble stalls one vessel on cavitation), **3 leaf section ×200** (a freeze-fractured block: cuticle, upper epidermis, palisade packed with chloroplasts, lobed spongy mesophyll round large air spaces, a minor vein, the lower epidermis and a stoma over its substomatal chamber; water leaves the vein, `AirSpaceVapour` drifts to the chamber and out through the pore, and a boundary-layer sheet thins with wind) and **4 stoma ×800** (the lower epidermis face-on: jigsaw pavement cells, two kidney-shaped guard cells with a cuticular ledge, a second stoma; K⁺ ions, K⁺/H₂O arrows that reverse under ABA, vapour diffusing out of the pore). Every mesh is our own, modelled in Blender by `scripts/plant-model` and loaded from `public/models/transpiration.glb` (4.8 MB, 13 nodes) through `plant-model.jsx`, which unpacks the 16-bit positions to floats once and makes the sRGB vertex colours linear. What moves with the model is three.js: the seedling sways in the wind in its vertex shader (`_sway`, `uWind`); its blades get a tertiary vein net and a paler underside in the fragment shader (`_uvl`); it wilts in drought (morph `wilt`, eased towards `0.55 + 0.4 × light` when the soil is dry); both stomata open with the aperture (morph `open`); the soil water films thin in drought (morph `dry`); and the soil pales and its top cracks (`uDry`). The stem's tissues are drawn per pixel by `makeStemMaterial` from the ring radii and vessel positions in `plant-model-meta.js` (a 3D Voronoi per tissue, stretched along the stem where its cells are long, open vessel lumens on the cut end). A **Focus** choice (`focus`: whole / roots / stem / leaf / stoma) flies the camera (`FocusCamera`) to one panel and turns on that panel's detail labels; the whole view keeps only the titles and the status labels. `lib/transpiration.js` is steady state: light → K⁺ uptake → turgor → aperture (ABA residual under drought), Tetens VPD, stomatal ‖ boundary-layer conductance, flux → mL/hr over 0.5 m² of leaf, cohesion–tension = −Ψsoil + ρgh + R·E with strained/cavitation thresholds. Every stream runs at the one flux; the water recolours sky → amber → rose with tension. Labels go through `LabelsOn` / `ToggleLabel`. No shadows.
   - **Peristalsis & Digestive Transit** (`peristalsis`): Our own 25 cm oesophagus and J-shaped stomach, modelled in Blender by `scripts/gut-model` and loaded from `public/models/gut.glb` (1.9 MB) through `gut-model.jsx` (it reuses `usePackedModel` from `plant-model.jsx`). The oesophagus has its front 110° cut away down its whole length, so the lumen, the bolus and every layer of the wall show: mucosa folded into longitudinal ridges, submucosa with glands and vessels, the inner circular and outer longitudinal muscle (bundled on the outside), a thin adventitia. The stomach has a fundus beside the cardiac notch, a greater curvature, an antrum, the pylorus and the start of the duodenum, with the gastric and gastro-epiploic arteries and veins along both curvatures and branches over the front. The wall is stored at rest and moved on the GPU: every vertex carries `_tube` = (station s, depth w into the wall, fold offset). Each frame `Gut` samples `profileRadius` over `lumenFeatures`, `layerActivation` and the shortening shift every `GUT_STEP` (0.25 cm) into `uProf`, and the vertex shader puts each vertex at the radius its station now has. Behind the bolus the wall closes and thickens (the contracting ring) and the mucosal folds crowd together; ahead of it the wall opens and thins and the folds flatten; the segment ahead slides together as it shortens. The fragment shader draws the layers from w (stippled circular fibres, streaked longitudinal ones, a wet, glossy mucosa) and flushes the circular layer rose and the longitudinal layer amber where each contracts. `scripts/gut-model/wave_preview.py` poses the same wave in Blender for checking. The three wave labels are edge-aligned: "behind" and the bolus hang off the left of the tube, "ahead" off the right; the four layer labels sit by the right-hand cut face. `FitCamera` frames the whole gut, labels use `LabelsOn`, and there are no shadows.

   - **Shared timeline kit** (`lib/timeline.js`, `timeline-kit.jsx`): stages laid end to end; `describeTimeline(t)` gives stage, progress, fraction, complete; `TimelineDriver` restarts on a trigger counter (a change to 0 goes idle, which is what the HUD's Reset does), holds at the end, writes a snapshot ref every frame, calls `onTick` at 10 Hz, and — given `scrub: { value, onChange, step }` — pushes the playing time into the slider and treats any value it did not push as a user jump that pauses playback. Burst/spray effects are pure functions of the snapshot, so they allocate nothing at runtime; CDP buffer counts confirm every buffer each scene creates is freed on the topic switch.
   - **Mitosis & Meiosis — Spindle Mechanics & Crossing Over** (`mitosis_meiosis`): `divisionPose(mode, t)` in `lib/cellDivision.js` turns the stepper clock into 0–1 channels (condense, envelope, poles, spindle, align, separate, elongate, furrow, pair, crossover, chiasmaVisible, fade) and `choreograph` in the scene turns those into placements written into one live bag every frame from the driver's `onFrame`. The membrane is a dynamic `ProfiledTube` lathe of two sphere profiles under a smooth-max whose centres drift apart and whose neck sharpens with the furrow (`k` scaled by the centre gap, so a resting cell is exactly R); each of the eight chromatids is a dynamic `ProfiledTube` whose `centreAt` bows sisters into an X (`side · bow`), sweeps arms back into a V (`pullDir · bend`) and drags distal arms across to the homologue past a chiasma, and whose `colourAt` blends towards the other parent by `blendAt` (allocation-free `originAt`); the spindle is one `InstancedMesh` of unit cylinders (kinetochore fibres pole → `seat + pullDir·0.06`, interpolar fans past the equator, asters) with a `poison` ease that shrinks them to green stubs under colchicine. Meiosis II uses two `makeUnit` frames (spindle along y, chromosomes along x) and `unitOf` deals each chromosome to the pole its bivalent's assortment sent it to. The HUD's `stepper` control takes `stages: (p) => stagesFor(p.mode)` and `locked: (p) => arrestIndexFor(p.mode)`; the driver's lock runs a cell already past metaphase forward to the next one. `FitCamera` frames `DIVISION_VIEW` (the two daughter cells at their widest). Labels and the stage caption use `LabelsOn`. No shadows.
   - **Cardiac Cycle & 4-Chambered Heart Hemodynamics** (`cardiac_cycle`): `CARDIAC_CYCLE` is five fixed fractions of one beat; `beatTimeline(bpm)` gives their real durations (systole ∝ √RR, filling ≥ 12 % of the beat) and `tempoFor(bpm)` the per-stage rate the `StageCycleDriver` plays at, so the stepper can park on "ejection" at any rate while continuous playback keeps physiological proportions. `hemodynamicsAt(stage, progress, bpm, pathology)` is piecewise per stage and continuous at every boundary (isovolumetric holds keep volume fixed; ejection pressures ride a baseline from opening to closing pressure; the dicrotic notch is a dip, the incisura, then a rebound). Electrical events lead the mechanical ones: `ecgAt` puts the P wave at the end of filling (its tail runs into atrial systole) and the QRS at the end of atrial systole, so R comes before S1 (it used to peak inside isovolumetric contraction, after S1), and `conductionAt` fires the SA node late in filling, holds at the AV node through atrial systole (the PR interval) and sweeps His → Purkinje with the QRS.

     The heart is a real one: `public/models/heart.glb` (6.8 MB), baked offline by `scripts/heart-bake/` from BodyParts3D (© The Database Center for Life Science, CC BY 4.0; credit in `lib/heartCredits.js`, shown by the scene's **Credits** button and pinned by `tests/unit/heart-credits.test.mjs`).
     - **From the scan:** the four chambers' blood pools (voxelised at 1 mm; LV 97.9 cm³ against the scan's 97.0), the atrial walls, every valve leaflet and cusp, the papillary muscles, the coronary arteries and veins, and the great vessels.
     - **Modelled:** BodyParts3D has no ventricular myocardium, so the walls are grown around the real cavities at textbook thicknesses (LV 10 mm, RV 4 mm, atria 2.5 mm), the septum being where they meet. Epicardial fat is a morphological closing of the outer surface, so it fills only the grooves. Trabeculae ridge the ventricular linings.
     - **The section** is the four-chamber plane, fitted through the cavity centroids and turned so the long axis (apex → AV valves) points up and the right heart is on the viewer's left. The aortic and pulmonary valves really sit 13 and 30 mm in front of that plane, so, as in an atlas section, they and the great vessels in front of it are not drawn: blood streams into the outflow tracts beneath them, and the callouts point there ("→ aortic valve · body"). The GLB still carries that part as a `ghost` mesh, unused. It was drawn as x-ray glass, which read as a clutter of loose parts over the chambers. The opaque heart's vessel stumps are cut off in its fragment shader above rest y 3.0 and right of rest x 3.1, where the superior vena cava's and left pulmonary veins' ends otherwise floated as rings joined by thin strips of cut wall.
     - **Surface:** vertex colours come from the tissue (cut muscle, atrial muscle, fat, left or right endocardium, arterial or venous wall, epicardium, with ±7 % mottling; the palette is `HEART_TISSUE` in `heart-model.js`). The colour alpha carries wetness, which `wetTissue` turns into roughness (glistening linings and epicardium, a duller cut face).

     The beat moves the vertices in the vertex shader (`BEAT_VERTEX_BODY` in `heart-model.js`, fed by `beatUniforms` / `setBeatUniforms` each frame; `deformPoint` is the same sum in JavaScript for the few loose points). Doing the 120 k vertices in JavaScript took 35–66 ms a frame and made the beat stutter:
     - Each chamber scales about its centre with r′ = ∛(r³ + a³(s³ − 1)), so the wall keeps its volume and a squeezing ventricle thickens.
     - The AV plane descends up to 0.55 (about 11 mm) towards the apex in systole, and the atria stretch.
     - Stenosis pushes the LV wall out (hypertrophy), and fibrillation shivers the ventricles.

     The heart mesh carries baked weights (`_beat_a` = lv, rv, la, ra; `_beat_b` = descent, LV depth, vessel; normalised bytes). The shader reads them as `aBeatA` / `aBeatB`. Loose parts use `weightsAt`, the same fields approximated from chamber-centre distance: the coronary vessels get them as attributes once at load, and valve hinges, particles, conduction tubes and fibrillation seats are moved in JavaScript with `deformPoint`.

     `heart-model-meta.js` (generated) carries the chamber centres and cavity radii, apex and base, each leaflet's hinge, axis and free-edge points, the papillary tips, the vessel centroids, and the conduction system. The bake computes the conduction points by marching out from each chamber's centre through its real cavity to the wall, then snapping onto the surface, so the bundle branches lie on the correct faces of the septum. Only the tricuspid and mitral leaflets are drawn. `leafletPose` finds each one's hinge from its own shape (the rim furthest from the valve's axis) and turns it about the rim's tangent from the scanned pose to a shut angle (free edge meeting the axis) or an open one (along the flow, tilted slightly inward) by the model's open fraction. The bake's hinge sat at the valve's middle rather than on the ring, so a fixed turn about it swung the tricuspid out through the RV wall. Each leaflet has three chordae to the nearest papillary tip. Local clipping (`z ≤ 0`) keeps the leaflets, coronaries and conduction behind the section.

     The model is loaded with `useGLTF` inside its own `<Suspense>` ("loading the heart…"). Without that boundary the whole R3F tree suspended and remounted while the GLB streamed, and the canvas lost its WebGL context.

     `useLayout` puts the Wiggers panel beside the heart when the canvas aspect is above 1.2 and the callout columns (`CALLOUT_PX` wide in CSS pixels) take under a quarter of the width each, and under it otherwise. Beside the heart it widens the view and slides the panel right until both columns clear the canvas edge and the panel; with labels off it closes back in. The panel names each phase over the middle of its own span, with the two isovolumetric phases below it. Labels are `Callout` columns: the right heart and the conduction system on the viewer's left, the left heart on the right. `wiggersSamples` is memoised on (bpm, pathology) so trace geometries rebuild only then (`useTraceGeometry` disposes on change); the cursor and the ring-buffer `EcgStrip` are per-frame writes. `liveProgress` / `liveVfTime` are pushed at 10 Hz for the Details readout. `FitCamera` frames the layout's view. Every label, including the Wiggers panel's and ECG strip's tags, goes through `LabelsOn` (`ToggleLabel`), so **Show labels** off leaves the traces bare. No shadows.
   - **Shared stage-stepper kit** (`lib/stageCycle.js`, `stage-stepper.jsx`, HUD `stepper` control): a cycle wraps (`wrapTime`, laps) and every stage has a HOLD point; the pure stepper state `{ t, index, playing, lock, tween, arrested }` is driven by `stepperJump/Next/Prev/Play/Pause/Lock/Tick` (a tween is wall-clock and shortest-way-round; a lock is forward-only and clamps requests beyond it; `advanceCycle` splits a step at stage boundaries so each runs at its own tempo). `StageCycleDriver` reconciles the HUD's stage index and playing flag with the machine every frame, pushes the index back while playing (remembering what it pushed so the echo is never a jump), rebuilds when the cycle object changes (mitosis ↔ meiosis) and exposes `onFrame` for choreography plus `onTick` at 10 Hz. CDP counts: 120 / 120 buffers freed for the cell scene after a full meiosis lap plus an arrest, 373 / 373 for the heart after every pathology and rate.
